# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective Cornell students evaluating whether to apply, current students learning how the team works, sponsors and alumni checking team credibility, and competition-adjacent visitors looking for evidence that Cornell Electric Vehicles builds serious hardware. They arrive with limited time and need to understand the people, the vehicle, and the level of execution quickly.

## Product Purpose

The site presents Cornell Electric Vehicles as an ambitious project team building autonomous, efficient electric vehicles. Success means more strong applicants, more sponsor confidence, clearer public understanding of the team, and a stronger sense that CEV is a real engineering organization rather than a student-club brochure.

## Brand Personality

Precise, kinetic, collaborative. The voice should feel technically competent and human: rigorous enough for sponsors and engineers, welcoming enough for students who are deciding whether they belong.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic dark-tech landing pages, anonymous three-card project-team layouts, inflated startup copy, toy-like car imagery, and motion that feels ornamental. Do not make the team feel like a software startup with no hardware, and do not make the vehicle story crowd out the people building it.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the team as the engine: people, subteams, and craft should appear before the page becomes a vehicle spec sheet.
2. Make the vehicle tactile: use real photography, dimensional layouts, and scroll-linked changes that make the engineering feel physical.
3. Keep Cornell red as a disciplined signal: use it for action, focus, and identity, not as a wash over every surface.
4. Show proof through real outcomes: competition results, vehicle lineage, sponsor logos, and team scale should be visible without fake precision.
5. Respect motion sensitivity: any scrolltelling or 3D-inspired motion must degrade cleanly for reduced-motion users.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-accessible navigation and CTAs, descriptive image alt text, and reduced-motion fallbacks for animated sections. The recruiting story should make interdisciplinary membership clear across engineering, data, autonomy, operations, and communications.
